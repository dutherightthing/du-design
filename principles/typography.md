# Typography principles

## The essentials
- **Two typefaces max, often one.** A display/heading face + a workhorse body face is plenty. One well-used family beats a pile of fonts.
- **Set a type scale and obey it.** Pick a handful of sizes (e.g. a modular scale ~1.2–1.33 ratio) and don't freestyle in between. Consistency of scale is most of what "designed" looks like.
- **Hierarchy through contrast.** Distinguish levels with clear jumps in size/weight/color — not tiny 1px differences. If two levels look similar, they read as the same level.

## Pairing
- Safe, high-quality default: one characterful display face + one neutral, highly legible body face (a clean sans or a readable serif).
- Contrast the pair (e.g. serif display + sans body, or a distinctive display + neutral sans). Two similar fonts fight; two contrasting fonts harmonize.
- When unsure, one great sans used at multiple weights covers most jobs.

## Legibility
- Body copy: comfortable size (16px+ on web), line-height ~1.4–1.6, line length ~60–75 characters.
- Don't set long text in all-caps, thin weights, or low contrast.
- Headlines can be tighter (line-height ~1.0–1.2) and larger; give them room.

## Personality (ties to intake sliders)
- **Serious/Modern:** clean geometric or neutral grotesque sans; tight, precise.
- **Playful:** rounded sans, higher weight contrast, more character.
- **Retro/Textured:** display faces with era-appropriate flavor (grotesques, slab serifs, script), used sparingly.
- **Editorial/premium:** a good serif for headlines signals craft and authority.

## Practical
- Prefer variable fonts for weight flexibility without extra files.
- Establish spacing: consistent margins above/below headings; don't let text touch edges.
- On the web, load fonts efficiently (subset, `font-display: swap`); avoid layout shift.
